## Summary

- Akhor Khel is a learning app for children aged 3 to 8.
- **The developer does not collect any personal information.** There is no account, no sign-up,
  no server, no analytics and no chat.
- Children's progress stays **only on the device**, and a parent can delete it at any time.
- The app is free and shows **ads from Google AdMob**. Ads are requested as **child-directed and
  non-personalised**, limited to general-audience (G) content. To show ads, Google's ad software in
  the app sends some technical data to Google, described below.

## 1. Information stored on the device

The app saves the following **on the device only**. It is never sent to the developer or anyone
else by the app:

- child profiles: a nickname and a chosen avatar picture (no real name, age, photo or other
  personal detail is asked for);
- learning progress: stars, attempts and completed letters;
- settings: language, sound, left-hand mode and similar;
- ad counters (number of app launches and the time of the last full-screen ad), used only to limit
  how often ads appear.

The child's tracing is used only on the device to give stars and is never sent anywhere. The app
does not use the camera, microphone, contacts, location or files.

**Teacher export.** In teacher mode (behind the parent gate), an adult can create a PDF or CSV of
class progress (nicknames and stars). The file is created on the device and only leaves it if the
adult chooses to share it using Android's share menu, to an app and recipient the adult selects.

## 2. Information processed by Google AdMob

When the device is online, the Google Mobile Ads SDK inside the app requests and shows ads. Google
states that this SDK collects the following, for advertising, analytics and fraud-prevention
purposes, and that it is encrypted in transit:

- the device's **IP address**, which may be used to estimate the device's general (approximate)
  location;
- **interactions with ads and the app**, such as app launches, ad taps and ad video views;
- **diagnostic information** about the app's and the SDK's performance, such as launch time;
- **device identifiers** such as the app set ID and, where applicable, other identifiers.

What we do to protect children:

- every ad request is marked as **child-directed**, which tells Google to turn off
  interest-based (personalised) advertising and remarketing for that request;
- only **non-personalised** ads are requested, with a maximum ad content rating of **G**;
- the app **does not use the Android advertising ID**: the permission that gives access to it is
  removed from the app, and child-directed requests do not transmit it;
- the app uses only Google AdMob, which is on Google Play's list of Families Self-Certified Ads
  SDKs, and no other advertising, analytics or tracking software;
- no full-screen ad is shown when the app opens, and no ads at all are shown before a parent has
  seen the ads notice or during the first session after install;
- a small banner appears only on the home screen, letter grid, word list and parent area, never
  while a child is tracing or playing a learning game; there are no rewarded or "watch an ad to
  unlock" ads.

Google processes this data under its own policies. Learn more:

- How Google uses information from apps that use its services:
  <https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites>
- Google Privacy Policy: <https://policies.google.com/privacy>

The developer does not receive these data from Google except as aggregated, non-identifying
reports (for example, total number of ad impressions and earnings).

If the device is offline, no ad requests are made and every lesson still works.

## 3. Children's privacy

Akhor Khel is designed for children and is built to meet Google Play's Families policy.

- We do not knowingly collect personal information from children, or from anyone. The app has no
  way to send the developer any information about a child.
- We do not track children across apps or websites, build profiles of them, or show them
  personalised or behavioural ads. The ad data described in section 2 is processed by Google for
  child-directed, non-personalised ads only.
- Features meant for adults (settings, teacher mode, sharing and links that leave the app) are behind a parent gate.
- **United States (COPPA):** the app is intended to be handled as a child-directed service. Ad
  requests are tagged for child-directed treatment so that Google applies its COPPA-related
  restrictions. The developer does not collect personal information from children.
- **India (Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025):** the developer does
  not process children's personal data and does not undertake tracking, behavioural monitoring or
  targeted advertising directed at children. Ads are requested in child-directed, non-personalised
  mode. Before the children's obligations under the Rules apply, the developer will review this
  policy and the app with legal advice, and update both if anything more is needed.

Parents with questions can contact us at the email below.

## 4. Deleting information

Because the developer holds no information about you or your child, there is nothing for us to
delete on our side. Parents can remove the on-device information at any time:

- in the app: Parent area → delete a child profile, or Delete all data (profiles, progress, settings) on
  this device;
- in Android: Settings → Apps → Akhor Khel → Storage → Clear storage; or
- by uninstalling the app.

App backups are switched off, so progress is not copied to cloud backups by the app.

For data held by Google in connection with ads, see Google's privacy controls at
<https://policies.google.com/privacy> and <https://myaccount.google.com/>.

## 5. Permissions

The app asks Android for only two permissions, both granted automatically and used only by the ad
software: **Internet** and **network state** (to know whether an ad can be loaded). It does not
request location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage, notifications or the advertising ID.

## 6. Security

There is no server or database to breach. Data on the device is stored in the app's private
storage. Communication between the ads SDK and Google is encrypted (TLS).
